Day-of teardown · Topgolf Charleston · 10 October 2026

Check #96

Two people, one bay, noon to 1:30. The check came to $111.92. Before tax, 59% of it was food and drink. Here is what Topgolf held at each step of the day, and what it did with it.

Prepared for David McKillipsBy Neil GolsonCharleston, SC · Bay 111 · 12:00–1:30 PM ET

The visit

The booking teardown stopped at the confirmation page. This one starts at 8:26 on Saturday morning and runs to the follow-up email on Sunday afternoon.

The money moved the way the model says it should. The weak spots sit around it. My wife’s invite hit an error screen. A server sold both beers, the first one up, then told me the membership had no advantage. The in-bay order page took a food order in under five minutes, then the app showed a $45 offer that doesn’t run on Saturdays. The family next door couldn’t get their game on the bay TV.

I recorded the screens on both phones, took photos and kept notes all day. Every finding below comes with the screen or photo behind it.

The findings · 12 to 23

12

My wife’s invite hit a Try Again screen.

The app sign-in took nine seconds. The greeting was up in three, the waiver with its one button in eight, and “You’re in! Now, let’s get your group signed in” in twelve. That screen also offers Invite Friends and Add Junior Player. The app works.

The invite didn’t. My wife got the text: “Let’s go to Topgolf Charleston! I just invited you to play at Topgolf Charleston.” The first screen she saw was a membership fee. Then Try Again under a yellow exclamation mark, at 10:34, 10:36 and 10:37. No reason, no phone number, no second button.

Her registration failed at the submit page every time. She checked and unchecked the email and text boxes in every combination, and every one landed on Try Again. After she gave up, I had the idea to download the app and try there. That worked. By 11:19 the reservation read Players 2/6.

My notes, 9:27 AM

“Still no text. Can’t register 2nd person. No game day notification, email.”

The Try Again screen offered no second route. The one that worked came from me, not from the screen.

What it costs

A second named guest on a paid booking is a cheap lead, and the invite is the one moment Topgolf asked for one. Nothing on my phone told me she was stuck. The number to pull is invite completion: sent, opened, registered, retried. A casino books a companion in one step, at the desk, with staff to finish it.

13

Every text before noon was logistics. The bay controls came ten minutes out.

Between 8:26 and 11:51 I counted seven touches: one email, one reservation screen, three texts, the 11:44 game-day card and the 11:50 bay controls. The email asked for a 25 to 30 minute video conversation with Topgolf’s research partners and offered “a free 2-hour bay reservation” for it. The three texts said: here is your check-in link, reply Y to confirm and we’ll text you your bay number, and “Bay 111 is ready! Please head there now to get the fun started.” None carried an offer. Only the 11:44 card sold anything, and it named no game.

The reservation screen at 10:25 still read Players 1/6 and still carried the Thursday copy about a text on the day of your reservation. The text did come, 90 minutes before the bay. That is a good window for the invite. Topgolf has a clean slot to ask who is coming and what they want, and it spent the slot on a link.

The controls ran the other way. The in-app bay controls (Tap to order, Food & Drink, TV Remote, Games, Your check) showed up at 11:50, ten minutes before the bay opened. We were driving and almost there. That’s tough timing if you actually want me to set things up in advance.

The 90-minute window is the invite. The 10-minute window is everything the guest can actually choose. A hotel treats the hours before arrival as selling time. The guest is committed and has not spent yet. Confirm, offer the upgrade, offer the dinner, send the late checkout. None of that came.

What it costs

Ninety minutes out is a cheap moment to sell food. The guest has not arrived, nobody waits behind them, and no tab is open. Topgolf sent a link. Ten minutes out, in a car, is the worst moment to ask anyone to use an app, and it is when the bay controls appeared.

14

The $6 membership fee is named three different ways.

My wife saw it first, before she could register: “A $6 one-time fee will be added to the check for each membership created. Memberships are required to play and provide access to game scores, leaderboards and more! Is this for you or a Junior Member under 13?” The button reads ME.

At 11:58 the bay screen shows it again as “Lifetime Membership (x1) $6.00”, next to Game Cost $36.56, and an “Amount added to bill” of $42.56. On the printed check it is line one, “Membership.” The same $6, labelled a one-time fee, a lifetime membership and a membership.

The first thing a new guest reads is a price. A minute later, she was looking at Try Again.

One $6 membership line landed on our table’s check.

What it costs

A $6 charge at the step where a stranger decides whether to bother is a filter on capture. It may be the right filter. The invite funnel is the number that says what it costs.

15

A server sold both beers, the first one up. The phone took the food order.

The printed check has two beer lines: a Coors Light 22oz at $7.00 and a pitcher with two glasses at $20.99. That is $27.99, or 46% of the food and drink on the check. The app order had no beer in it, just food and a soda.

A server came by right away and took my drink order. I asked for a Coors Light, and she sold me the 22oz for $2 more. My wife was in the restroom. When she came back, we ordered food and her drink on the app. Very easy. For my second beer I ordered a pitcher from the same server. The app sells beer. She sold it better, more conveniently and at better times. The 11:44 app card had already promised “22oz drafts (21+).” The server was the one who sold it.

The order page did its job. I scanned the QR code on the table at 12:03 and it opened on “Welcome to Topgolf” with the bay number, 111, in large type. It didn’t say my name, which was confusing. A minute later the menu was open on Apps, with a “Pay my tab, TAB OPEN” banner up. At 12:07 the review screen read “Me (3)”: Crispy Chicken Strips $12.79, Hot Honey Pepperoni Flatbread $15.79, Dr Pepper $3.99. At 12:08, “Your order is in!” The order came to $32.57, start to finish in 4 minutes and 44 seconds.

The food was on the table by 12:24, inside sixteen minutes. It was great. The hot honey pizza was hot and full of flavor. The chicken tenders were moist. It came fast.

The review screen carries a Log in link in the top right corner, so the order may sit outside my Topgolf account. I’m not sure what logging in would have done.

The refill went the other way. At the one-hour mark my wife wanted another drink and no server came. We ordered from the QR code, and it came pretty quickly.

What it costs

Both beers went through a person, the first with a $2 step up. The app sells beer, and the server won on timing and ease. When she was missing at the one-hour mark, we waited, then ordered from a card. A casino host works the floor with the player’s record in hand. Nothing I saw suggests this server had one.

16

The app showed a $45 offer at 12:16, on a Saturday it does not run.

At 12:16 the app showed a card that read: “Food & Fun, All in One. Get two entrees + 1.5 hours of game play for only $45!” The size fits two people. The order was in at 12:08 and the bay had run 16 minutes. Below it sat a Happenings card, “GOLF. BEER. FOOTBALL.” It is the banner from the confirmation page in finding 07 of the booking teardown, now in the app on a football Saturday. That teardown also shows the $45 combo on Thursday, under “No offers yet.”

The in-bay home screen at 12:00 led with ordering and pay-from-phone, with no offer. The combo sat on a different screen.

It isn’t valid on Saturday. It runs Monday through Thursday, from 4 PM to close, and those terms sit in the app one tap behind the card. The app knows what day it is. It showed me the offer anyway.

The card is the right size for two, and we could not have used it. The card shows no day or hour limit, and it sat on my phone at noon on a Saturday.

What it costs

A casino host does not show a player a comp the player can’t redeem. This card sets up a price the house won’t honor on Saturday, and the guest can’t tell from the card. I saw no two-person bundle valid on a Saturday. Show the guest what is valid today, and show the right one first.

17

The TV remote worked before we got there. Nothing told me what to put on it.

First, what worked. As we parked and walked in, I could control the bay TV from the app. One option was ESPN, and UNC at Pitt was the noon game on ESPN. I set it before we got there, and it was on when we arrived. Very nice. That’s the product doing what it says.

Now, what I had to bring myself. At 11:51 the app’s Bay 111 TV Remote listed seven channels, each with a show title: CBSSN, Inside College Football; ESPN2, SportsCenter; MLBN, MLB Tonight; ESPN, College GameDay (highlighted); WTAT, Big Noon Kickoff; NHL, On the Fly; WCBD, Yes, Chef!

At 11:54 my phone was in the ESPN app. It showed “Watch live: College GameDay,” then UNC at 25 Pitt at 12:00 PM on ESPN, with a spread, a total and a moneyline. A second noon game, Texas A&M at Missouri, sat below it on ABC.

The remote changes the channel. It does not say what kicks off at noon. I had to know UNC and Pitt was on ESPN, and at 11:54 my phone was in ESPN’s app. Nothing in Topgolf’s screens told me. The 11:44 card sells “Golf. Beer. Football.” and tells guests to “control your bay’s TV with the Topgolf App.” It does not say which game or which channel.

What it costs

A game-day guest decides the afternoon at kickoff: the pitcher, the second round, whether the bay gets extended. At that moment the screen is in someone else’s app. Topgolf is the venue and the remote. The selling happens one app over. The family in finding 22 shows what that costs when the game is not on the list.

18

Two extension prompts, neither with a price. The follow-up came the next afternoon.

The bay screen read “15 minutes left” over “Great news! More play time is available in this bay. Would you like to extend?” Two buttons: No Thanks and Let’s Extend. No price, no length, nothing about food.

At 7 minutes left a server came by to extend or close out, and the check closed at 1:23. Two asks, one from the screen and one from a person, and neither named a price. More play time was available in the bay, and nobody put a number on it.

The next touch came on Sunday 11 October at 2:59 PM, about 25 and a half hours after the check closed: “Tell Us About Your Visit & Get 30 Mins on Us.” The email prints the date as 2026-10-10. It asks for a survey, two to three minutes, and nothing else.

Both asks pay in play time. The Saturday morning one asked for 25 to 30 minutes on video and paid 2 hours. The Sunday one asked for 2 to 3 minutes and paid 30. Two hours is a unit the booking flow sells. Thirty minutes is not (booking teardown, finding 04).

What it costs

Post-visit is the first moment Topgolf can ask a guest back. The only follow-up was a survey, a day late, paid in a unit the booking flow does not sell. A hotel sends the folio and a rebook offer the morning after.

19

The server told me the membership had no advantage.

Topgolf sells PlayMore at the front door. The sign in the 11:56 photo reads “Members get more: Free Game Play, Free Appetizers, Exclusive Perks.” The sale is made to a stranger walking in.

My check had a $6 membership line on it, the fee in finding 14. As I read the plan, PlayMore waives that $6 and includes an appetizer every visit.

At 7 minutes left I asked the server about it.

What the server told me

No advantage, because I hadn’t gotten an appetizer. Get it next time.

The chicken strips I ordered at 12:08 sit under Apps on the order page. I don’t know whether her rule excludes them or whether the answer was wrong. I’m not criticizing her. I don’t know what she was given to say.

What it costs

The check was open, the guest asked, and the answer was no. That is the highest-intent moment of the day for a membership. A casino enrolls the player at the machine or the table, with the card in hand, and the host is paid on the sign-up. Topgolf’s door sells the plan and its own server pushed the sale to next time. Nobody owns that moment.

20

Toptracer was in the bay. My guest went to YouTube for the lesson.

My wife, a first-time player, sat down halfway through the first game and watched a YouTube video on how to swing. Golf tips. Such an opportunity.

Here is where Toptracer shows up. On the bay TV, in the 12:09 photo, a strip reads “STATS by TOPTRACER” with Carry, Ball Speed and Hang Time. The label is small. Under it sits a How to Play tile. That tile is the game rules, not golf tips.

That label on the TV is the only place Toptracer showed up all day. It wasn’t in the app or the order flow. In my inbox it appears in one place: the 8 October coupon terms, “Not valid at Lounge, Swing Suite, or Toptracer Range locations.”

What it costs

The one lesson she looked for came from YouTube. The bay never offered her one. That is Toptracer’s value proposition walking to a competitor’s platform, mid-visit, with a first-time guest. One guest does not make a segment. It is the moment a range product earns its keep.

A game-day party whose game was not on the bay’s channels. They came in team colors on a college football Saturday, to a venue whose app sells “Golf. Beer. Football.” The game they wanted was on in the bar, and their bay TV ended up on Texas A&M on ABC, because South Carolina wasn’t on the bay’s stations.

A second screen. The dad’s phone sat propped on their table with South Carolina on it (photo below). I’d used my phone for the same job at 11:54, checking a kickoff time.

What it costs

One bay, three misses, none on the check. A casino host who sees a family in team colors on game day makes the room theirs: the right channel, a comp on the cake, a name in the file. This family could not get their game on the bay TV, and a phone propped on the table did the job. Birthdays are an occasion with a date. A date can be sold a year ahead.

23

Three men in their twenties priced Topgolf against a $7 range.

The bay on our left held three men in their twenties. I’m not showing or naming them.

What they told me

They said maybe once a year. If it weren’t raining they would have gone to the driving range instead, because “it’s like $7.” They called Topgolf expensive.

A once-a-year guest sets Topgolf against the range, and the range wins on price. By their account rain made the decision. Weather gave Topgolf this table.

What it costs

Demand that arrives with the rain is a rain check. It leaves when the sun comes out, and nothing I saw in the bay was built to change that. A casino treats the once-a-year visitor as a rated guest with a next-visit offer. It’s the price-value gap at its cheapest comparison.

Method

How I put this together
