The insight

Every one of these audiences had already been told they were obsolete

Realtors have heard since the first day of the internet that they wouldn't be needed. Parking operators heard the same thing about automation. Home improvement retail heard it about e-commerce. By the time you show up with a platform, the audience has a decade of practice reading you as the threat.

That's the actual starting position, and it rules out most of what B2B marketing does by default. Feature messaging lands as a replacement argument. Discounting reads as a race to commoditize them. Loyalty programs treat the relationship as a rebate.

What works is making the professional more valuable in the transaction they already control. Give them exposure they can't buy, credentials they can't self-issue, or a customer conversation they couldn't have started alone. Then the platform stops being the thing that displaces them and becomes the thing that funds them.

"When realtors succeed, Realtor.com succeeds."

The three companies below are the same play run against three different industries. Real estate is a two-sided marketplace where agents are the supply. Parking was a category with no brand and no language. Energy needed a retail channel it didn't own. Same method, different terrain.

Realtor.com · The argument

The consumer campaign is the B2B proof point

The National Association of Realtors created Realtor.com 30 years ago to connect consumers to real estate professionals rather than route around them. That origin is the whole asset, and it's the one thing Zillow and Homes.com can't copy. The B2B film exists to put it back in front of the industry in the agents' own words, through NAR leadership and working brokers.

Here's the part that reframes the brand budget. Every dollar of the $50M consumer campaign buys exposure that lands on the listing agent, and agents don't pay for it out of dues dollars. The Expert Edge positioning makes the agent the expert. Reba sells the necessity of the person who knows the market.

So the Reba work does double duty. Aimed at consumers it drives traffic and trust. Aimed at the industry it's a retention argument for the supply side of the marketplace, which is the half of the business that decides whether there's anything to sell.

The advocacy work runs the same logic at the category level. Let America Build put us alongside Habitat for Humanity, ICON Homes, NAHB, Veterans United, and Zillow on housing supply. Sitting at a table with your largest competitor on a problem neither of you can solve alone is a credibility move you can't buy with media. We also co-sponsor the Good Neighbor Award, Fair Housing Champions, and the Realtor Relief Foundation, which is where the industry watches whether you mean it.

Realtor.com · The B2B engine

An industry audience needs a full go-to-market, not a trade booth

The argument only converts if there's a machine behind it. I rebuilt the B2B product architecture and rebranded the portfolio so a broker could understand what they were buying and how the tiers related, which the prior naming actively worked against.

RealPro Select is the elite tier that came out of that work, built to compete directly with Zillow Flex for the agents who drive disproportionate volume. Around it sits ongoing product marketing and a run of B2B launches, each one needing its own positioning against a competitor with more money.

Underneath that, a demand gen creative pipeline: paid social, the demand gen CRM program, webinars, and the sales collateral the field actually carries into broker meetings. This is the unglamorous half, and it's the half that decides whether the brand argument reaches a decision maker or dies in a booth conversation.

Trade is where the industry takes your measure in person. At NAR NXT we ran a 40×80 footprint in front of 12,000 Realtors. The International Builder Show covers the other side of the supply equation, which is where the Let America Build thesis and the builder relationships meet. I also took the mainstage at the Top 100 Brokers and Top 100 Builders event on building trust and engaging consumers in today's media landscape, which is the version of this argument that gets made to the people who own the rooftops.

Flash · Category creation

Give an industry the language it's missing and it will carry your brand for you

Parking operators ran a real business inside a category with no brand, no shared vocabulary, and buyers who thought they were making a procurement decision. Their own customers, commercial real estate owners, treated them as a line item.

Naming the category and building the Mobility Hub architecture gave operators a story to tell their landlords. That's the same move as agent exposure: the operator walks into a CRE conversation worth more than they were the week before, and the platform is why.

The trade press did the distribution. In a B2G and commercial real estate category, buyers read the trades and google vendors before every meeting, so the IPMI cover and the booth program did real work. They're how a category with no brand found out it had one.

Tesla · SolarCity · Winning a channel

The partner's floor associate is the audience

SolarCity needed homeowners at the moment they were already thinking about energy, and it didn't own a store. Home Depot did, 800 of them, and the person who decided whether solar came up was an associate in the plumbing or lumber aisle.

So the channel strategy pointed at that associate. Branded endcaps in lumber and garden, a trained energy specialist in each location, and a 1,500-person field org built to support them. I'd managed a Home Depot store earlier in my career, which is why the program got designed around how the floor actually works instead of how a partnership deck imagines it.

The result of getting the channel right shows up in the numbers on that case study: national residential solar share moved from 26% to 37%, and install volume tripled.

What generalizes

The method, in the order it has to happen

Find what the professional can't buy for themselves. Exposure, category language, a credential, a customer at the right moment. That's the only currency that outruns a discount, because a competitor can always pay more than you.

Point the consumer brand at them. The most persuasive B2B asset is usually the consumer work, because it's proof you can generate demand they'd otherwise buy themselves. Brand spend reads as a cost center until the supply side sees it as their lead flow.

Build the tiering before the loyalty program. Professionals sort themselves by volume and ambition. RealPro Select works because it's a status ladder with economics attached, which beats a rebate.

Show up where they take your measure. Trade floors, mainstages, and the trade press are where an industry decides if you're serious. A 40×80 booth in front of 12,000 Realtors is a credibility instrument.

Get caught doing something that doesn't pay you. Coalitions and advocacy, Let America Build, the Relief Foundation, Fair Housing. An industry can tell the difference between a sponsorship and a stake.
