Realtor.com · NAR NXT 2026 · New Orleans · Nov 6–8

Ten thousand Realtors, one city, and Reba on the main stage

NAR NXT is the one week a year the whole real estate industry is in the same building. Agents are the supply side of our marketplace, and the consumer campaign only pays off if they believe in it. So we took the campaign to them: Reba in front of a maxed-out General Session, a 50×80 booth on the floor, and New Orleans painted in one line from the airport to the convention center.

The room

The consumer campaign has to win the agents too

Reba sells consumers on the person who knows the market. That only works if the agents she's selling believe Realtor.com is on their side, and they've spent 30 years hearing that portals want to replace them.

NAR NXT is where that belief gets tested in person. Brokers, team leads and the agents who drive most of the volume all walk the same floor in the same three days, and they decide which platforms are serious by who shows up and how.

So the goal was simple to say and expensive to fake: make the industry's biggest week feel like it ran on our campaign.

The General Session

Four thousand Realtors in white hats

Reba took the General Session stage on Saturday morning for a fireside chat with Justin McIntosh. The room maxed out at 4,000.

We put white hats in the seats. When she raised hers, the whole hall did the same, under a Realtor.com logo. That's the photo at the top of this page.

The session was built around strategy and storytelling for agents. Our spokesperson, on their stage, talking about their job.

Painting the town

From baggage claim to the main stage, one line

Attendees met the campaign before they met the convention. In the airport concourse, the consumer work was already running: "Going places? Let a local agent get you home." At baggage claim the line switched to the agent: "Be the expert who stands out" ran on the screens there, on a wallscape and billboards around the convention center, on banners along the walkways, and on the shuttle buses and escalators in between.

The Wall Street Journal's NAR NXT edition carried a Realtor.com cover wrap, with a commemorative poster inside. By the time an agent badged in, they'd already passed Reba on the way, and every time she was talking about them.

The booth

A 50×80 booth that pointed at the agent's own business

The booth carried the same line and turned it into product. Agent profiles with RealReels, Spotlight Listings with iGUIDE and Realtor.com+ were on the wall, each one framed as a way for an agent to stand out to the consumers Reba was sending.

Completing your agent profile was the entry to the sweepstakes, so the giveaway did product work: meet-and-greets with Reba, merch drawings through the weekend, and an iGUIDE camera. Reba walked the floor and spent time at the booth.

We also honored the Realtors Relief Foundation's 25th anniversary at NXT Fest with a $100,000 check. An industry notices which brands show up for it when there's nothing to sell.

What generalizes

An event is where the brand argument gets made in person

The biggest opportunity at a trade show is to make the whole week an extension of the brand your customers' customers already know.

For a marketplace, that means pointing the consumer campaign at the supply side. Agents saw the spokesperson their clients watch on TV, on their stage, in their city and in our booth, pointing at the tools that put them in front of those clients. That's the B2B case for brand spend, made without a single slide.
